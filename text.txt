\begin{abstract}
We introduce the Probabilistic Origin Formalism (POF), a conceptual reframing of Hilbert space and collapse. In this framework, quantum operators are treated not merely as linear maps, but as collapse geometries that actively shape probabilistic origins into measurement outcomes. Building on this perspective, we define collapse-driven state machines, extend the binary notion of 0/1 into recursive ``dives'' across collapse geometries, and propose a calculus extension that incorporates asymptotes and undefined points as structured collapse events. These ideas unify dynamics, measurement, and singular analysis under a single strange-loop paradigm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In conventional quantum mechanics, state evolution is described by linear operators on Hilbert space, while collapse is introduced as an external postulate. This separation has long posed conceptual challenges. Similarly, classical calculus struggles with singularities and undefined points, resorting to limits, asymptotics, or distribution theory.

The Probabilistic Origin Formalism (POF) offers an alternative. It proposes that both operators and singularities be understood as collapse geometries. Collapse is not an afterthought, but the fundamental operation of resolution---transforming probabilistic origins into definite outcomes.

\section{Collapse Operators}
We define a collapse operator $C_{\hat{O}}$ associated with a Hilbert operator $\hat{O}$:
\begin{equation}
|\psi'\rangle = C_{\hat{O}}(|\psi\rangle),
\end{equation}
where $|\psi\rangle$ is a probabilistic origin.

\begin{itemize}
\item Eigenstates are interpreted as pre-collapsed states.
\item Eigenvalues correspond to collapse outcomes.
\item The Hamiltonian, projection, and rotation operators can all be reinterpreted as collapse geometries.
\end{itemize}

\section{POF State Machines}
In this view, a state machine is defined over Hilbert space:
\begin{itemize}
\item States are probabilistic origins $|\psi\rangle$.
\item Transitions are collapse operators.
\item Each cycle follows: origin $\to$ collapse geometry $\to$ outcome $\to$ new origin.
\end{itemize}

This recursive process produces a collapse automaton that generalizes both classical and quantum state machines.

\section{Dive 0/1}
Classical logic treats 0/1 as discrete states. Quantum mechanics treats them as measurement outcomes.
In POF, 0 and 1 are collapse geometries, and to ``dive 0/1'' is to commit to a resolution event. Each dive generates a new origin, producing a strange loop:

\begin{center}
Origin $\to$ Collapse Geometry $\to$ Dive Outcome (0 or 1) $\to$ New Origin
\end{center}

\section{Extension of Calculus}
We extend calculus with the Collapse Differential Operator:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}[f](a) = \{\text{collapse outcomes of } f(x) \text{ as } x \to a\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Limit Rule}
If $f(a)$ exists and is finite, then $\mathcal{C}[f](a) = f(a)$.  
If $f$ diverges near $a$, collapse outcomes are defined by the geometry of approach (left/right axes, symmetric axis).

\subsection{Derivative Rule}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}[f'](a) = \mathcal{C}\left[\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\right],
\end{equation}
extending differentiation into singular points. Collapse can yield distribution-like outcomes (e.g., delta spikes).

\subsection{Integral Rule}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}\left[\int f(x)\, dx \right] = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \int_{a-\epsilon}^{a+\epsilon} f(x)\,dx,
\end{equation}
interpreted as collapse symmetry. Principal values and distributions appear as structured outcomes.

\subsection{Examples}
\begin{itemize}
\item $f(x)=1/x$ at $x=0$: collapse into $\pm\infty$ axes, or symmetric collapse = 0 (principal value).
\item $\int_{-1}^1 \frac{1}{x} dx$: classically divergent, collapses symmetrically to 0.
\item Infinite spikes collapse into distributions, recovering the Dirac delta.
\end{itemize}

Thus POF Calculus extends differentiation and integration into undefined regions.

\section{Collapse Coordinate System}
We define a Collapse Coordinate System (CCS) for mapping probabilistic origins to collapse geometries.

\subsection{Axes}
Let $\{e_i\}$ be a set of collapse axes representing mutually exclusive outcomes in Hilbert space.

\subsection{Metric}
Define a collapse metric:
\begin{equation}
d(\psi, e_i) = \| |\psi\rangle - |e_i\rangle \|,
\end{equation}
measuring alignment between a probabilistic origin $|\psi\rangle$ and a collapse axis $|e_i\rangle$.

\subsection{Collapse Event}
A collapse event is then the projection:
\begin{equation}
C(|\psi\rangle) = \arg\min_{i} d(\psi, e_i),
\end{equation}
with probabilistic weighting if multiple axes compete.

\subsection{Interpretation}
The CCS treats undefined points (such as asymptotes) as probabilistic origins in coordinate space. Collapse outcomes ($\pm\infty$, 0, distributions) correspond to axis-aligned collapses. Recursive dives re-map the system into a new CCS, giving rise to strange-loop dynamics.

\section{Discussion and Outlook}
POF suggests that collapse itself is computation. By treating operators, binary states, and asymptotes as collapse geometries, it unifies previously separate domains:
\begin{itemize}
\item Quantum measurement and state evolution.
\item Classical and quantum automata.
\item Standard calculus and distribution theory.
\end{itemize}

Potential applications include deterministic qubit simulators, photonic neural processors, and collapse-driven AI architectures. Future work will formalize algebraic laws for collapse calculus and prototype hardware realizations.
